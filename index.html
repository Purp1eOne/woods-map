      Measure & walking time

      This is a straight line, not a trail route. Real walking through brush, water or hills usually takes longer.

      How to use Woods Map

      First time

      Open the app on wifi and allow location when it asks. Tap Locate at the bottom left. The blue dot is you. The pale circle around it shows how accurate the GPS is. It is smaller in the open than in thick woods.

      Maps and layers

      Tap Layers at the top right and pick Topo (contour lines), Satellite, Satellite + trails, Hybrid or Street. Satellite + trails is the clear satellite photo with the Topo map's trails drawn in orange. Hybrid adds road and trail names and faint contour lines, but it shades public land such as wildlife refuges a pale white. Both have the same trails as Topo, not every path in the woods. Switch on MN DNR public land to see Minnesota Wildlife Management Areas, or MN walk-in access for southwest Minnesota. The map covers Minnesota and Illinois; Illinois has the same Topo, Satellite and Street maps but no public-land overlay yet. Street needs a signal. Topo, Satellite, Satellite + trails and Hybrid can be saved for use offline. Satellite + trails takes about twice the space, since it saves two sets of images.

      Adding pins

      • Pin me drops a pin where you are standing.
      • Pin center drops a pin at the crosshair. Pan and zoom the map first. This is how to mark a spot you are not standing on. The numbers at the top left are the crosshair position.
      • Pressing and holding on the map also starts a pin.

      Give it a name, a type and notes. There are about 25 types, grouped into stands, deer sign (scrapes, rubs, beds, trails, funnels, sightings, sheds), food and water, turkeys and ducks, and access (truck, gate, entry route, property corner, hazard). Pick a color too, or leave it on the type's own color. Colors are handy for sorting spots by year or by who found them. Tap Add photo to take or attach a picture, such as a trail cam shot or a big rub. A range ring draws a 30 to 200 yard circle around the pin. Trail cameras and harvests also keep a date, and cameras show how long ago you checked them.

      Lines and areas

      Open Pins and tap Draw a line or Draw an area. Then tap the map to add points. You can also line up the crosshair and tap ✚ Crosshair, which is easier to place exactly. Drag a point to move it, and Undo takes back the last one. A line shows its length and walking time, which is handy for entry routes, deer trails and property lines. An area shows its acres and the distance around it, for bedding areas, food plots, CRP or the land you have permission on. Tap Done, or for an area tap its first point. Then give it a name, color, group and notes.

      Tap a line or area on the map to edit it, move its points, share it or hide it. They're listed under the pins in Pins, and Show on map can hide all lines or all areas. Share sends a KML file that Woods Map, Google Earth, onX and most map apps can open.

      Using your pins

      Tap a pin on the map for Go there, Edit and Share. Share sends the pin by text, email or any app, with its photo if it has one. The message has a Google Maps link and a Woods Map link. A friend with Woods Map who opens that link gets the pin ready to save, in a group called "Shared with me". Tap a pin's photo to see it full screen. The Pins button lists every pin, nearest first once GPS is on. You can search, or tap Edit on any row to change or delete a pin.

      Groups and hiding pins

      Every pin, line and area belongs to a group, like a folder. Your own pins start in My pins. Type a new name in the pin editor's Group box to start another group, such as "2025 season". In Pins, open Show on map to hide or show any mix of pin types, and to switch whole groups off or on. To hide just one pin, tap it and choose Hide, or tick Hide this pin on the map in the editor. It stays in the Pins list, greyed out, and tapping it there shows it again. Lines and areas hide the same way, from their own Hide button or editor. Hidden pins aren't deleted, and the Truck button skips them. A yellow chip at the top tells you when pins are hidden. Tap it to show everything again.

      Finding your way

      • Go there shows a yellow bar with distance, walking time and compass direction, plus a dashed line on the map. Tap the ✕ to stop.
      • The Truck button starts Go there to your nearest pin of type Parking / truck.
      • In Pins, Measure gives distance and time between any two points.

      Times are straight-line at the pace you set under More. Brush, water and hills make a real walk longer, so use a slower pace for rough ground.

      Recording your walk

      Tap Track, then Start recording. It records the path you walk so you can follow it back out. The screen stays on while it records, and it only records while the app is open. Saved walks can be shown, hidden, deleted or exported as GPX.

      Sunrise and shooting hours

      The chip at the top shows sunrise and sunset for where you are. Tap it for the big game / deer shooting window, which is 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset in both Minnesota and Illinois. Other seasons have different hours, so check the current regulations.

      Insights: the rut and your deer seasons

      Tap Insights on the bottom bar for the whitetail rut phase on any date you pick: pre-rut, seeking, chasing, peak or post-rut. It also shows how long until the next phase, what deer are doing, and where to hunt. The peak, November 10 to 16, comes from Minnesota DNR breeding research. The other phases are estimates. Tap Why these dates? for the sources.

      Below the rut, Insights uses your GPS to find the Minnesota deer permit area you're in and shows its archery, firearms and muzzleloader dates, straight from the DNR's permit-area data. Each season shows whether it's open on the date you picked, and which rut phases it covers. Looking up a new area needs a signal, but the last one is saved for when you're out of service. Always confirm with the current DNR regulations.

      Coordinates

      Tap the coordinates at the top left to copy them, or to type in a spot and jump there or pin it. It reads 46.3045, -94.2001 and also N 46° 18.270 W 94° 12.006.

      Before you leave: save maps for offline

      1. Do this on wifi. Pan and zoom so the screen covers the area you hunt.
      2. Tap More, then Offline maps. Choose a map and a detail level, then Download this area.
      3. Repeat for the other map if you want both.
      4. Turn on airplane mode and check that the map still shows.

      Your pins, tracks and GPS dot work with no signal. Saved maps live on your own phone, so each person downloads their own.

      Backup and sharing

      Under More, Export pins (backup) saves a file of your pins, lines and areas, with their colors and photos. Import pins loads one (a Woods Map backup, GPX, or KML from Google Earth and other map apps, including their lines and areas) and never changes or removes your own pins. It asks which group to put the new pins in, such as "Mike's pins", so you can hide them all in one tap. When restoring your own backup, leave the group empty to keep your groups as they were. To share spots, send the file to a friend and have them import it. Export now and then, since pins live only on this phone.

      Good to know

      • GPS under thick cover or in a deep valley can be off by 20 to 30 yards or more. When you drop a pin, wait for the circle to shrink.
      • Public land boundaries are for reference. Private land is not shown. Confirm access rules and ownership before you hunt.
      • GPS and Track use extra battery, more so in the cold.
      • Pick Dark, Light or Match my phone under More.

      Insights

      Whitetail rut (Minnesota)

        A planning guide, not a forecast. Day length sets when does breed. Weather, the moon and hunting pressure change how much deer move in daylight, not when the rut happens.

        Why these dates?

        The peak is the best-supported part. The Minnesota DNR's White-tailed Deer Handbook (2000) puts the midpoint of the rut in the week of November 12, when about half of does 1½ years and older have been bred. Northern Minnesota research from 1983–87 (Fuller 1990, cited in a 2005 DNR report) estimated peak conception at November 10–14. The DNR says deer breed from November into early December.

        The other phase boundaries are estimates that split the season into the phases hunters use. They are not official DNR dates. Minnesota hasn't published separate breeding dates for the north and south, so the same calendar is used statewide.

        The second rut note comes from the handbook too: a doe that isn't bred comes back into heat about 28 days later, and some doe fawns breed in December.
